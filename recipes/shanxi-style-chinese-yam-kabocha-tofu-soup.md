# Shanxi-Style Chinese Yam, Kabocha & Tofu Vegetable Soup

A light, savory Chinese-style vegetable soup with **铁棍山药 (iron-stick Chinese yam)**, kabocha, tofu, fresh shiitake, corn, daikon, and carrot. This version does not require pork, but optional dried scallops add extra umami.

**Makes:** about 6–8 servings  
**Total cooking time:** about 1¼–1½ hours

## Ingredients

### Main ingredients

- 10 cups water
- 1 lb (about 450 g) iron-stick Chinese yam (铁棍山药)
- 1 lb (about 450 g) kabocha squash, about ½ medium kabocha
- ½ medium daikon, about 8–10 oz
- 2 medium carrots
- 2 ears corn
- 14 oz (400 g) extra-firm tofu
- 10–12 medium fresh shiitake mushrooms, about 6–7 oz
- 5–6 thin slices fresh ginger

### Optional aromatics / umami

- 4–6 small/medium dried scallops (干贝 / 瑶柱)
- 2 scallions
- ¼–½ tsp white pepper
- ½–1 tsp sesame oil

### Seasoning

- 1½ tsp salt to start, plus more to taste
- Optional: 1–2 tsp light soy sauce if the broth needs more savory depth

## Preparation

### 1. Optional dried scallops

If using dried scallops:

1. Rinse them briefly.
2. Soak in warm water for 20–30 minutes.
3. Keep the soaking water; add it to the soup if it is clean and free of grit.
4. The scallops do not need to become completely soft before cooking.

If you are not using dried scallops, simply skip this step.

### 2. Chinese yam

Wear disposable gloves if possible because raw Chinese yam can irritate the skin.

1. Rinse the yam.
2. Peel off the brown skin.
3. Cut into roughly 1½-inch chunks.
4. Keep the pieces in cold water while preparing the other vegetables.

### 3. Kabocha

1. Cut the kabocha in half.
2. Scoop out the seeds.
3. Leave the skin on.
4. Cut into roughly 1½-inch chunks.

The skin becomes tender when cooked, so there is no need to peel it.

### 4. Other vegetables

- **Daikon:** Peel and cut into roughly 1-inch chunks.
- **Carrots:** Peel and cut into roughly 1-inch chunks.
- **Corn:** Cut each ear into 3–4 chunks.
- **Fresh shiitake:** Wipe/rinse clean, remove woody stems, and slice the caps into roughly ¼-inch slices.
- **Ginger:** Slice 5–6 thin pieces.
- **Scallions (optional):** Cut into roughly 2-inch pieces.

### 5. Tofu

Drain the extra-firm tofu and cut it into roughly 1-inch cubes.

No pressing is necessary.

## Cooking Instructions

### Step 1 — Build the broth

1. Add **10 cups water** to a large pot.
2. If using dried scallops, add the scallops and their clean soaking water.
3. Add the ginger.
4. If using scallions, add them now.
5. Bring to a boil.
6. Reduce to a gentle simmer and cook for **15–20 minutes**.

Do not use a hard, vigorous boil.

### Step 2 — Add the harder vegetables

Add:

- Corn
- Carrots
- Daikon
- Fresh shiitake

Bring back to a gentle simmer and cook for **20 minutes**.

### Step 3 — Add the Chinese yam, kabocha, and tofu

Add:

- Chinese yam
- Kabocha
- Extra-firm tofu

Gently stir once to distribute everything.

Simmer for **15–20 minutes**, checking the yam with a fork around the 15-minute mark.

The yam should be tender but still hold its shape. The kabocha should be tender; it may start to soften and break apart slightly.

Because the tofu is extra-firm, it can go in at this stage and will hold together well.

Keep the soup at a gentle simmer and avoid vigorous stirring.

### Step 4 — Season

Start with **1½ tsp salt**.

Taste the broth and add more salt gradually until it tastes pleasantly savory.

If desired, add:

- ¼–½ tsp white pepper
- ½–1 tsp sesame oil

If the broth still needs more savory depth, add **1–2 tsp light soy sauce**.

Avoid making it overly salty; the soup is intended to be light and clean-tasting.

### Step 5 — Finish

Once the yam and vegetables are tender:

1. Turn off the heat.
2. Taste one final time and adjust salt.
3. If using fresh scallions but did not add them to the broth, sprinkle them over the soup.
4. Serve warm.

## What the Finished Soup Should Taste Like

The goal is:

**savory → slightly sweet → earthy → clean**

- Shiitake and optional dried scallops provide umami.
- Corn, kabocha, and daikon provide natural sweetness.
- Ginger adds warmth.
- Yam and tofu make the soup more substantial without requiring meat.

## Storage & Meal Prep

Let the soup cool before portioning.

For single-serving containers, aim for **16–24 oz per portion**. Try to distribute the tofu, mushrooms, yam, kabocha, and other vegetables evenly between containers.

- **Refrigerator:** about 3–4 days
- **Freezer:** Freeze individual portions for longer storage.
- Reheat until hot throughout.

For freezing, leave a little space at the top of each container for expansion.
