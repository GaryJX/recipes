# Clear-Broth Tofu & Poached Egg Noodles
## 清汤豆腐荷包蛋面

A light, comforting Chinese noodle soup with thick wheat noodles, tofu, bok choy, and poached eggs. The seasoning is intentionally mild and works well for a northern-Chinese/Shanxi-style home meal.

**Servings:** 3

## Ingredients

### Main
- 12 oz fresh thick wheat noodles — ideally 刀削面, 手擀面, or another thick fresh wheat noodle
- 1/2–1 block firm tofu, about 7–10 oz
- 3 eggs
- 1 large bunch bok choy
- 2–3 scallions

### Broth
- 6 cups chicken stock
- 3–4 thin slices fresh ginger
- 1 small garlic clove, lightly smashed — optional
- 1 1/2 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1/2 tsp salt to start
- 1/2 tsp sesame oil
- Tiny pinch white pepper — optional

> **Tip:** If your chicken stock is already fairly salty, start with only 1 tbsp soy sauce and no additional salt. Adjust at the end.

## Instructions

### 1. Prepare everything

**Tofu:** Cut the tofu into roughly 1/2-inch cubes. You don't need to press firm tofu.

**Bok choy:** Separate the leaves and rinse well. If the bok choy is particularly large, cut the stems into smaller pieces and leave the leaves whole.

**Scallions:** Slice thinly and set aside for serving.

### 2. Make the clear broth

Put the following into a medium/large pot:

- 6 cups chicken stock
- 3–4 slices ginger
- Smashed garlic, if using

Bring to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for **8–10 minutes**.

Remove the ginger and garlic.

Add:

- 1 1/2 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1/2 tsp salt

Stir and taste.

The broth should taste slightly under-seasoned at this point. The noodles, tofu, and vegetables will dilute it a little.

### 3. Add the tofu

Add the cubed tofu to the broth.

Keep it at a **gentle simmer for about 5 minutes**.

Avoid a vigorous boil so the tofu doesn't break apart.

### 4. Poach the eggs

Turn the broth down to a **very gentle simmer**. You should see small bubbles, but not a rolling boil.

Crack one egg into a small bowl first, then gently slide it into the broth. Repeat with the other two eggs.

For a **soft yolk**, cover the pot and cook for approximately **3–4 minutes**.

For a **firmer yolk**, cook for **5–6 minutes**.

Don't stir the soup while the eggs are cooking.

Once they're done, you can leave them in the pot or carefully transfer them to a bowl temporarily.

### 5. Cook the noodles separately

While the eggs are cooking, bring a separate pot of water to a boil.

Cook the noodles according to their package directions. Fresh noodles may only take **2–4 minutes**.

Err slightly toward al dente rather than overcooking them.

Drain the noodles well.

> **Important:** Don't cook the noodles directly in the soup. Fresh wheat noodles release quite a bit of starch, which will make the clear broth cloudy and thicker.

### 6. Cook the bok choy

Bring the soup back to a gentle simmer.

Add the bok choy stems first and cook for about **1–2 minutes**.

Then add the leaves and cook for another **30–60 seconds**.

You want them tender but still vibrant and slightly crisp.

### 7. Assemble

Divide the noodles between **3 bowls**.

Add tofu and bok choy.

Carefully place **one poached egg** into each bowl.

Ladle the hot broth over everything.

Finish each bowl with:

- A few sliced scallions
- A **very small drizzle of sesame oil**
- Tiny pinch of white pepper, if desired

Taste the broth once more and add a tiny amount of salt or soy sauce if needed.

## What the Finished Soup Should Taste Like

The goal is a **delicate rather than intensely seasoned** soup:

> savory chicken broth → chewy wheat noodles → soft egg yolk → mild tofu → fresh bok choy → subtle scallion and ginger aroma

Avoid adding chili oil, vinegar, oyster sauce, five-spice, or lots of soy sauce. The simplicity is part of the dish.

## Optional Adjustment

If the chicken stock is particularly bland, add another **1/2 tsp soy sauce + 1/4 tsp sesame oil** at the very end rather than making the whole broth stronger from the beginning. This gives you more control over the final flavor.
